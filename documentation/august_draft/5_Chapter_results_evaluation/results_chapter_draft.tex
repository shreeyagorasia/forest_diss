> The higher seed variability provides evidence of optimisation instability. A mismatch between the pooled CR derivative and local growth is a possible explanation that requires comparison with observed plot-level changes.

### Short answer to Question 2

For example:

> The Chapman–Richards constraint altered optimisation and reduced some measures of physics violation, but it did not produce a consistent predictive improvement and increased sensitivity to the training seed.

Only retain the physics-violation statement if the results demonstrate it.

## 5.4 Question 3: Which conditions are associated with persistent departure from the shared CR curve?

This is Avenue 1.

### 5.4.1 Distribution and spatial pattern of shared-curve residuals

First establish what is being explained:

* distribution of plot-level mean CR residuals;
* map of positive and negative residuals;
* residual variation between compartments;
* Moran’s (I);
* semivariogram or spatial range, if useful.

Explain whether departures from the pooled curve are:

* substantial;
* persistent across surveys;
* spatially clustered;
* large enough to justify attribution modelling.

This connects the prediction results to the attribution analysis.

### 5.4.2 Can the residual departures be predicted?

Compare:

* intercept or mean baseline;
* Elastic Net;
* XGBoost;
* mixed-effects model, if retained.

Present:

* held-out MAE, RMSE and (R^2);
* spatial cross-validation results;
* fold variation;
* observed-versus-predicted residuals.

The important question is not simply whether XGBoost fits the training data. It is whether the departures are predictable in spatially held-out areas.

### 5.4.3 Which variables are associated with the departures?

Present:

* Elastic Net coefficients;
* XGBoost SHAP importance;
* SHAP dependence plots for a small number of important variables;
* grouped importance by terrain, climate, wind and management;
* sensitivity after removing correlated or questionable variables.

Interpret:

* whether linear and nonlinear models identify similar variables;
* whether directions of association are credible;
* whether coarse variables mostly represent spatial zones;
* whether relationships remain after spatial validation;
* whether importance may be distributed between correlated predictors.

Use associational language throughout. SHAP explains the fitted model, not the ecological cause of growth.

### 5.4.4 Remaining unexplained spatial structure

After attribution, compare Moran’s (I) before and after modelling.

Ask:

* did environmental and management variables reduce spatial clustering?
* did substantial spatial structure remain?
* are there identifiable areas of consistently unexplained growth?

This is stronger than merely listing important SHAP variables because it tests whether the variables actually account for the spatial pattern.

### Short answer to Question 3

For example:

> The available predictors explained part of the persistent departure from the shared CR curve, but held-out performance was limited and residual spatial structure remained. The identified variables should therefore be interpreted as spatial associations rather than causal drivers.

## 5.5 Question 4: Which conditions align with plot-specific growth-curve deviations?

This is Avenue 2 and should be presented as more tentative because local curves are fitted from only four or six observations.

### 5.5.1 Reliability of the plot-level curve target

Before presenting attribution models, show whether (\Delta y_{\max}) is reliable enough to analyse.

Include:

* number of eligible plots;
* number or proportion of failed local fits;
* distribution of local (y_{\max});
* frequency of implausible or boundary estimates;
* effect of the disturbance filters;
* comparison between four- and six-survey estimates;
* uncertainty or fit error.

### 5.5.2 Global attribution models

Compare:

* Elastic Net;
* XGBoost;
* simple baseline.

Present:

* spatially held-out performance;
* variation between folds;
* feature importance or coefficients;
* comparison with Avenue 1.

Ask whether environmental and management information can explain the more ambitious trajectory target, rather than only the shared-curve residual.

### 5.5.3 Does allowing spatially varying relationships help?

Present the GNNWR results:

* performance against Elastic Net and XGBoost;
* global versus spatially varying relationships;
* maps of selected local coefficients;
* stability or plausibility of those coefficients;
* remaining residual spatial structure.

Do not treat attractive coefficient maps as proof of improved attribution. First establish that GNNWR generalises adequately and produces reasonably stable relationships.

### Short answer to Question 4

For example:

> Plot-specific curve deviations provided a more local description of growth, but their estimation from four or six observations introduced substantial uncertainty. The resulting attribution was therefore weaker than, or complementary to, the shared-curve residual analysis.

## 5.6 Overall synthesis

This section should be short—approximately one to two pages.

Bring the four questions together:

1. Which model predicted height most accurately?
2. Did adding CR physics help?
3. Could environmental and management information explain persistent shared-curve departures?
4. Did plot-specific trajectory modelling improve attribution?

A possible overall story is:

> Data-driven models captured spatial variation not represented by a pooled Chapman–Richards curve. However, imposing the selected Chapman–Richards constraints did not consistently improve prediction and increased training instability. Environmental and management variables accounted for some persistent spatial departure from the shared curve, although important spatial structure remained. The plot-specific trajectory approach provided a more local target but was limited by the small number of observations available for fitting each curve.

This synthesis prepares the reader for the final Conclusion chapter. The conclusion then returns to the objectives, states the contribution and explains the broader implications without repeating every result.
