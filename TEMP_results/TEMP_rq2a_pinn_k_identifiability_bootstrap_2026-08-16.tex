## TEMP holding note — PINN_k y_max/k cluster-bootstrap CI, current tier, pooled 5-fold (2026-08-16)

**Why this exists**: RQ2a item 3's corroborating bootstrap CI for the y_max/k identifiability
correlation was cited as `95% CI [-0.77, -0.62]`, flagged in the draft itself as "still the old,
pre-restructuring tier — not yet rerun current." A search of every file in `TEMP_results` for
these numbers found no source at all — this CI could not be traced to any saved result. Recomputed
here on the current tier instead, on the exact same pooled 5-fold w=0 predictions that produced the
flagship -0.9345 point estimate (`TEMP_rq1_physicsablation_results_2026-08-11.tex`'s "What does the
-0.935 correlation actually look like" section).

**Method**: `models/pinn_env_terrain_k/bootstrap_correlation_check_kfold_pooled.py` — pools the 5
per-fold `predictions.csv` files from
`outputs/spatial_block_kfold/rq1_pinn_env_terrain_k_nested_set3_gated_terrain_wind_vif_w0_seed42/`,
dedupes to one row per plot (matching the existing pooling approach exactly), then reuses
`bootstrap_correlation_check.py`'s own `cluster_bootstrap_correlation_ci()` unmodified (2000
resamples, resampling whole compartments with replacement, not individual plots — same
cluster-bootstrap convention already established elsewhere in this project). No new fitting.

**Run as**: `python -m models.pinn_env_terrain_k.bootstrap_correlation_check_kfold_pooled`

### Results

|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|

**4survey**: the CI is far tighter than the old-tier number it replaces ([-0.9437, -0.9232] vs. the
untraceable [-0.77, -0.62]) and never crosses zero across 2000 resamples — the near-perfect
anti-correlation is not small-sample noise, on the current tier, with a real saved source.

**6survey**: newly computed here for the first time (the old-tier CI was 4survey-only). The sign
flip reported elsewhere (+0.430 point estimate) is itself statistically robust — the CI
[+0.254, +0.589] stays positive throughout, confirming 6survey's opposite-sign correlation is a
real, reproducible pattern on this cohort, not noise scattering around zero either. 6survey's CI is
noticeably wider than 4survey's (relative to its point estimate), consistent with 6survey's smaller
sample (13,769 vs. 58,073 plots, 47 vs. 232 compartments) — the same sample-size-driven precision
gap seen throughout this project's 6survey results.
