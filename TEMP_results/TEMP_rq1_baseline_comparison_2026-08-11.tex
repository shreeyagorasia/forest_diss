### Headline finding

**On 4survey, DNN edges out RF/XGBoost, but PINN/PINN_k lose to them.** DNN (0.655±0.016) sits
just above RF (0.638±0.010) and XGBoost (0.637±0.013) — the SDs barely overlap at the edge, so
this is a real if modest edge, not a clear-cut win. PINN and PINN_k (0.573-0.575) are clearly
*worse* than both tree-based baselines here — the extra architectural complexity doesn't pay for
itself on 4survey once physics conditioning is added, consistent with this project's earlier,
now-answered physics-ablation finding (physics constraint hurts 4survey accuracy).

**On 6survey, all three neural models (DNN, PINN, PINN_k, 0.684-0.686) clearly beat both
baselines** (RF 0.656, XGBoost 0.651) — a real, unambiguous margin (roughly 0.03, outside the
baselines' own SDs). Neural conditioning earns its keep on 6survey regardless of which neural
architecture is used.

**Linear is comfortably the weakest model everywhere** (0.509-0.580) — expected, included mainly
as a lower-bound reference, not a serious competitor.

**Overall read**: the case for using a neural model over a simple baseline is stronger on 6survey
than on 4survey — on 4survey specifically, only DNN (not PINN/PINN_k) actually justifies the
extra complexity over RF/XGBoost. This is a real, citable nuance for the RQ1 chapter's "why a
neural model at all" framing, not a blanket "neural beats baseline" claim.
